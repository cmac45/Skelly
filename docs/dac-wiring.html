Skelly DAC Wiring

How to connect the PCM5102A DAC board to the ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 (N16R8) so it becomes Skelly's music player: spooky sounds and ambient loops on the ground speaker, through the amp's right channel. Six wires and four solder blobs. Do all of it with everything unplugged.

What you need on the bench

  • The ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 board and the PCM5102A board (usually purple, with a 3.5 mm jack on one end).
  • Six female-to-female jumper wires. If the boards came with loose header pins, solder those on first.
  • A soldering iron and a little solder, for the four jumpers on the back of the DAC.
  • A USB-C cable for the ESP32-S3. It has two USB-C ports: use the one labelled COM (or UART) for the first setup on the Mac.
  • A USB charger for the ESP32 out at the skeleton. A 2-port charger can power both it and the reSpeaker.

Your board is an ESP32-S3, so it has no GPIO26. The S3 numbers its pins differently from the classic ESP32 this guide first showed. Use GPIO5, GPIO6 and GPIO7. On the DevKitC-1 they sit together on the header beside the 3V3/RST end, and the silkscreen usually prints just the number (5, 6, 7), not "GPIO".

Step 1Set the four solder jumpers on the back of the DAC

Turn the DAC over. You'll find four little groups of three pads, marked H1L to H4L. Each middle pad must be joined to either the H side or the L side with a blob of solder. Some boards arrive already bridged; check each one anyway.

XSMT is the number-one cause of silence. Left on L, or not bridged at all, the DAC stays muted, and everything else can be wired perfectly with no sound coming out.

Also on the front, beside the SCK pin, many boards have one more small pad pair. Bridge it, or run the sixth jumper wire from SCK to GND (Step 2 shows this). Grounding SCK tells the DAC to make its own clock, so the ESP32 needs only three signal wires.

  • Keep the wires short, ideally under 15 cm. Long clock wires cause clicks and hiss.
  • Don't swap in other pins on a whim. On the N16R8, GPIO35, 36 and 37 belong to the memory chip, and GPIO0, 3, 45 and 46 affect booting. GPIO19 and 20 are the USB port. If you do use different pins, tell Claude so the firmware matches.

Step 3Connect the audio to the amp

The DAC's 3.5 mm jack is the music output. It shares the amp with the reSpeaker: Skelly's voice on the left channel, music on the right.

Start with the amp's volume low. The PCM5102A puts out a strong line-level signal, louder than a phone's headphone jack, so turn up from low rather than down from loud.

How it's all powered

Two supplies live in the weatherproof power box at the outlet; only low-voltage cables run to the electronics box. The DAC doesn't need its own supply: the red wire takes power from the ESP32-S3's 3V3 pin, which its USB supply feeds. (The board's 5V pin isn't reliably powered from USB on these N16R8 boards, which is why the DAC stays on 3V3.)

The amp's 12 V must never reach the ESP32 or the DAC. It destroys both instantly. That barrel plug goes to the amp and nowhere else.

  • Power the ESP32 one way only: through its USB port. Don't also feed its 5V pin from another supply.
  • Plug both supplies into the same outlet. That's the best defence against hum.
  • A long USB cable is fine for the ESP32; it draws only 0.1–0.25 A.

Step 4Check before you power up

Then plug the ESP32 into the Mac and tell Claude. Claude installs the music-player firmware, adds it to Home Assistant, and plays a test sound. Only then connect the amp.

If it doesn't work

Silent, and touching LOUT/ROUT makes a hum
The cable and speaker are fine, but the DAC is switched off: it has no power or no clock. First check the red wire is on 3V3 (this was the fix on Skelly's board), then the yellow and blue clock wires.
No sound at all
Check the XSMT jumper first: it must be on H. Then check that SCK is grounded and that BCK and LCK aren't swapped.
Clicks, crackle or hiss
The clock wires are too long or loose. Shorten them and reseat the jumpers.
A steady hum
That's a ground loop. Power everything from the same outlet first. If it's still there, add the ground loop isolator on the DAC's 3.5 mm cable.
Distorted or harsh
Turn the amp down. The DAC's output is strong.
The ESP32 won't start after wiring
A signal wire is on a pin the ESP32-S3 uses for booting. Check that yellow, blue and green are on pins 5, 6 and 7.
